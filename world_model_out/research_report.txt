Executive summary
Your current Malmo setup sits at the intersection of three research threads: learned visual world models,
short-horizon planning over latent dynamics, and modular visual navigation with explicit goal grounding.
Structurally, it is closer to PlaNet and TD-MPC than to Dreamer : you encode RGB frames, roll out
candidate futures in a latent space, and use a value-like term to score short action sequences. Dreamer is
more relevant as a next-stage reference if you eventually want to learn  policy/value updates inside
imagination rather than rely on exhaustive  candidate search. PlaNet is the clearest model-based
planning precedent; TD-MPC is the clearest precedent for short-horizon latent MPC plus a terminal value
head . 
The strongest conclusion from the literature is that for a small partially observable environment like env3
with 64×64 RGB , 3 turning actions , and H=4, your biggest practical bottleneck is probably not latent rollout
itself. It is more likely goal grounding under visual shift : night lighting, edge blobs, stale hypotheses, and
single-view false positives. RobustNav shows that embodied navigation agents can degrade sharply under
visual corruptions and low-light changes; work on low-light vision and corruption robustness shows that
brightness shifts, blur , and sensor-like artifacts can break detectors unless robustness is explicitly
engineered through augmentation, adaptation, or enhancement. 
If you only implement three changes next, the literature suggests a very clear ordering. First, add temporal
confirmation, edge rejection, and stale-hypothesis timeout  around the HSV+blob detector; these are
cheap, high-leverage fixes that directly target the failure modes you described and are conceptually aligned
with probabilistic mapping and tracking. Second, evaluate with multi-start, day/night, and detector-
ablation protocols  using navigation metrics such as Success Rate  and SPL rather than relying only on
offline reconstruction or rollout MSE. Third, if classical detection remains brittle, move to a lightweight
learned detector/classifier  or an open-set grounding model, and calibrate its confidence with uncertainty
methods so MPC stops overcommitting to weak visual evidence. 
A final theme is architectural direction. If your immediate goal is a stronger report and a better env3
pipeline, you do not need to jump straight to a giant end-to-end Dreamer-style system. The literature
supports a staged progression: start from your current modular stack, make the detector and hypothesis-
management robust, add a simple belief map, and only then decide whether to replace the deterministic
autoencoder/dynamics pair with a more task-oriented recurrent latent model such as PlaNet, Dreamer ,
SLAC, or TD-MPC-style latent control. That staged path is much closer to how successful navigation systems
such as Active Neural SLAM and Goal-Oriented Semantic Exploration combine learning with explicit maps
and planners. 3H

How the literature maps to your Malmo stack
The closest conceptual decomposition of your system in the literature is: visual encoder → latent state →
latent dynamics rollout → terminal scoring/value → MPC selection , with a separate detector and map
providing external goal evidence. That is a hybrid of world-model planning papers and modular visual-
navigation papers, rather than a pure end-to-end RL agent. PlaNet explicitly plans in latent space and
replans every step; TD-MPC uses a task-oriented latent dynamics model plus a terminal value function;
Active Neural SLAM and Goal-Oriented Semantic Exploration combine learned perception/mapping with
explicit planning over maps. 
Two architectural points from the literature are especially important for your setup. First, a representation
trained only for reconstruction is often weaker for control than a task-oriented latent state . PlaNet’s
recurrent state-space model, Dreamer’s imagined-value learning, SLAC’s stochastic latent control, and TD-
MPC’s task-oriented latent dynamics all emphasize that the best latent for acting is not necessarily the one
with the best pixel MSE. Second, short-horizon MPC gets much stronger when paired with a calibrated
terminal value . That is precisely the role your value head can play, but only if it is well-grounded and not
driven by detector false positives. 
For your specific env3 assumptions, exhaustive search over  action sequences is completely
reasonable. The literature does not strongly argue that you need deeper search first; instead, it suggests
that the more urgent improvements are better latent state quality , better goal evidence , and better
robustness to observation shift . In other words: in your current regime, improving night perception and
false-positive handling is likely to matter more than increasing H from 4 to 6. That is an inference from the
model-based RL and embodied-robustness literature, not a direct theorem, but it is well supported by how
performance collapses under corrupted input even when planning machinery is otherwise sound.

Methods most worth trying next
The lowest-cost, highest-return  step is to harden the current HSV+blob detector before changing the
world model. Add edge rejection  so detections whose centroids lie within a margin of the image border are
ignored or heavily down-weighted; edge artifacts are a classic source of false positives, and your own
observations about edge blobs point directly to this. Add temporal confirmation  so a new diamond
hypothesis must persist for  or  consecutive frames before entering the planner , and add a stale-
hypothesis timeout  so a target belief is cleared if it is not re-observed within  steps after a turn-back or
verification action. These are simple forms of probabilistic belief maintenance and tracking, aligned with
occupancy-grid reasoning and modern tracking-by-detection practice. 
The next cheap improvement is multi-view confirmation . When a first detection appears, do not
immediately commit the planner . Instead run a short verification macro such as left–center–right  or right–
center–left , and accept the object only if the bearing change is self-consistent for a static target. This idea is
strongly supported by probabilistic mapping and semantic-navigation systems: world evidence should get
stronger when multiple views agree, not when one crop happens to look “diamond-like.” If confirmation
fails, delete the hypothesis instead of letting the value head keep pulling MPC toward it. 
For the night failure specifically, test two perception fixes in parallel . The first is classical: adjust HSV
ranges or insert a tiny enhancement stage such as gamma correction or Zero-DCE before thresholding. The
second is learned: collect a small labeled day/night crop dataset and train a lightweight classifier to re-score
candidate blobs, using augmentation inspired by AugMix, ImageNet-C-style corruptions, and domain-
randomization logic. If the enhancement route helps but the learned route helps more, that tells you the
failure was not just brightness but a broader appearance-shift problem. 
If you move beyond HSV+blob, the most practical learned detector path  is not necessarily full open-
vocabulary grounding. For a single fixed category like diamond, a small detector or crop classifier may be
enough. Still, DETR is useful as a clean end-to-end detector reference, and Grounding DINO becomes
attractive if you foresee future object-goal variations or language-conditioned tasks. In either case, 
confidence calibration matters : deep ensembles or MC-dropout are straightforward ways to estimate
prediction uncertainty, and the planner can then multiply terminal value by confidence or reject low-
confidence detections entirely.

Recommended evaluation experiments for env3
The literature on embodied evaluation strongly suggests that you should shift from purely offline image-
prediction metrics to closed-loop navigation metrics . For env3, because the grid is only 11×11  and the
action set is small, you can compute exact orientation-aware shortest paths  in the ground-truth grid for
evaluation only. That means you can report Success Rate , SPL, and steps-to-success  exactly, rather than
approximately. 
Exact metrics to log
Use the following metrics for every evaluation condition:
Success Rate (SR) : fraction of episodes in which the agent reaches the goal region within 
steps. Define the goal region using ground-truth evaluator-only information, for example “agent
reaches the diamond cell or a 1-cell neighborhood,” depending on your mission design. 
SPL: success weighted by shortest path length, using the exact shortest number of action steps  in
the state space . Because turning costs are real in your action space, do not use
Euclidean distance alone. 
Steps to success : actual executed actions until success, averaged over successful episodes.
Collision rate : fraction of actions that result in collision or blocked motion.
False Positive Commitment Rate (FPCR) : number of times per episode the planner commits to a
goal hypothesis that is later invalidated by temporal or multi-view verification.
Time to first valid detection : steps until the first detector hypothesis that later survives
confirmation.
Detector precision/recall/F1 : on a held-out frame dataset, ideally at least 500 day + 500 night
frames from env3, with positives and hard negatives such as edge artifacts oversampled.
Night robustness gap : SR and SPL between day and night.
Fixed-start generalization gap : difference between fixed-start and multi-start SR/SPL.
Offline model quality : keep reconstruction loss and latent rollout error , but report them separately

Datasets and tools to study
Project Malmo  remains the right environment for your immediate experiments because it is built directly
on Minecraft and already matches your mission setup. Its official repository is useful not only for
environment access but also for instrumenting evaluator-only logs needed for your new metrics. 
Habitat-Lab  and Habitat-Sim  are the best tools to study for methodology. Habitat-Lab provides the task/
evaluation framework, while Habitat-Sim provides the high-performance simulator and common 3D
datasets. Even if you do not port your project, their codebases and metric conventions are excellent
references for reproducible navigation experiments. 
Gibson Env  is useful if you want a bridge toward more realistic indoor navigation and sim-to-real thinking.
It is built from real spaces and explicitly emphasizes transfer , which makes it a strong conceptual reference
for your report’s broader relevance section. 
CARLA  is less directly relevant to your 11×11 Minecraft world, but it is still worth studying if you want
examples of how the community treats environmental variation, weather , and sensor conditions as explicit
benchmark axes. That mindset is directly applicable to day/night Malmo evaluations. 
For robustness and visual grounding, the most useful external resources are RobustNav  for nav
corruptions, Zero-DCE  for a practical low-light preprocessor , and if you later train a learned detector ,
standard detection repos such as DETR  and Grounding DINO . 
Open questions and limitations
The main unresolved question is how much of your current closed-loop behavior is driven by true goal
grounding  versus geometric bias from a small map and limited start distribution . The recommended
oracle-goal and multi-start experiments are designed specifically to answer that. 
A second open question is whether your current latent state is actually limiting control. The literature
strongly suggests that task-oriented recurrent latents outperform plain reconstruction-oriented latents for
acting, but until you run the detector/perception ablations, it would be premature to blame the world
model first. In a system currently failing at night and on false blobs, perception robustness may still be the
dominant issue. 54
